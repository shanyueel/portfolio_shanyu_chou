  When a deadline can't move, I shift the conversation from **"when"** to **"what"**.

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuCalendarClock" title="The Constraint" variant="negative">
    The Data Warehouse project at WACA was tied to the company's pricing announcement, which was three months out. Sales and Marketing had already built their plans around that date, so pushing the schedule back wasn't an option. But the scope the PM had defined was too large to finish in time.
  </HighlightBox>

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuLayoutDashboard" title="Milestone-Based Breakdown" variant="neutral" className="mt-4">
    Once we confirmed the deadline was fixed, I worked with the designer and engineers to break the project into milestones, where **each milestone was a complete sub feature that could ship independently**. Then we sat down with the PM to prioritize: which features were essential for launch, which were less urgent and could safely move to the next development cycle.
  </HighlightBox>

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuRocket" title="The Outcome" variant="positive" className="mt-4">
    We delivered the Data Warehouse on time for the announcement, and the deferred features were shipped in the following cycle. The Data Warehouse became a core WACA feature: visualizing sales performance so our consultants could give customers better recommendations.

    The milestone-based approach became a standard practice in WACA, and it's still how I scope large projects and estimate development time.
  </HighlightBox>
