
<HeroCardContainer>
  During the development of Waca's data warehouse, I had to implement a complex Dual-Mode Date Picker with custom shortcuts and intricate permission logic.

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuPuzzle" title="The Challenge" variant="negative" className="mt-4">
    The biggest obstacle was that no third-party library met our specific design and functional requirements, and a brute-force implementation would have led to a 'spaghetti code' nightmare.
  </HighlightBox>

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuWrench" title="The Solution" variant="neutral" className="mt-4">
    I took a two-pronged approach:
    - **Design Negotiation**: I demonstrated the technical constraints of existing libraries to the design team, and adjusted the UI with them to leverage the library's strengths while keeping the most important features.
    - **Architectural Decoupling**: I broke the monolithic component into granular sub-components. Then, I abstracted the complex validation and business logic into a standalone Logic Controller (OOP approach). Therefore, I could instantiate a fresh 'state manager' everytime the picker opened, ensuring zero side effects.
  </HighlightBox>

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuRocket" title="The Result" variant="positive" className="mt-4">
    This decoupling made the codebase significantly cleaner and more testable. It allowed us to implement the feature on time, and reduced the maintenance effort for subsequent updates by roughly 30% because the logic was isolated from the UI.
  </HighlightBox>
