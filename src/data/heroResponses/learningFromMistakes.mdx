
<HeroCardContainer>
  In my early days at WACA,I once spent several hours building the wrong thing because **I didn't confirm the design before starting**.

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuTriangleAlert" title="The Mistake" variant="negative" className="mt-4">
    I was implementing a new multi-layer dropdown, and I assumed the sub-menu had to stay anchored next to its parent option. Since the parent list had its own scrollbar, the sub-menu drifted out of view whenever the user scrolled. I tried every approach I knew and still couldn't get a good experience.
  </HighlightBox>

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuSearch" title="What Actually Happened" variant="neutral" className="mt-4">
    Eventually I went to the designer to say the design was too complex to implement. He looked confused and show me the prototype: the sub-menu was simply **fixed and aligned to the top of the parent layer**. Far simpler than what I'd been working on. Once I understood it correctly, I finished the component almost immediately.
  </HighlightBox>

  <HighlightBox iconName="LuShieldCheck" title="The Habit It Created" variant="positive" className="mt-4">
    All those hours of struggle came from **one skipped step** — I never walked through the prototype before writing code. Now, whenever I pick up a new feature, I read the project docs and go through the prototype interaction by interaction to confirm every detail first. If I find any ambiguity, I clarify it with the colleague before writing a single line of code.

    It's a ten-minute habit that has saved me from repeating that mistake.
  </HighlightBox>
</HeroCardContainer>
