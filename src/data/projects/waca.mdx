## Technical Decisions

<Card>
  <TechnicalDecision title="Incremental Modernization via MPA Architecture">
    <TDContext>
      Our legacy PHP/jQuery architecture suffered from tight coupling, blurring development roles. Complex backend logic dominated, forcing frontend engineers into simple slicing roles. This "backend-driven" flow created major bottlenecks and an unbalanced workload. Furthermore, reliance on jQuery DOM manipulation resulted in fragmented code that was difficult to read, scale, or maintain for a platform with 2,000+ merchants and 50,000+ customers.
    </TDContext>
    <TDTradeOffs>
      We standardized on Vue 3 because it’s what the team knows best. The big question was how to integrate it: Inertia.js, a full SPA rewrite, or an MPA strategy. While Inertia.js is a Laravel package, it didn't align with our long-term goal of completely decoupling the frontend. A full SPA, on the other hand, was too high-risk. We couldn’t afford to spend months re-implementing core features like authentication, permissions, and payments while still having to deliver new business requests with zero downtime.

      Ultimately, we opted for an **MPA (Multi-Page Application)** approach. By embedding Vue 3 only into new features' pages, we immediately de-coupled the UI and sped up development. This strategic 'bridge' solution not only solved our immediate synchronization issues but also started the necessary shift toward API-first collaboration that prepared us for a future full SPA transition.

    </TDTradeOffs>
    <TDImpact>
      This transition effectively decoupled the full-stack architecture, allowing frontend and backend teams to work in parallel and **boosting overall development speed by 30%**. By leveraging Vue's reactive state management and component-based structure, we **eliminated 50% of redundant code** while centralizing logic and styles for much better maintainability.
    </TDImpact>

  </TechnicalDecision>
</Card>

<Card className="mt-4">
  <TechnicalDecision title="Independent Component Library (Storybook + Private Registry)">
    <TDContext>
      The legacy codebase lacked design guidelines, leading to messy styles and constant
      "reinventing the wheel." On the other hand, AI tools often generated custom layouts with raw
      Tailwind utilities instead of building components. This caused massive code duplication and a
      maintenance nightmare where simple UI updates required changes in dozens of places.
    </TDContext>
    <TDTradeOffs>
      To centralize styles and prevent design drift across multiple projects, we need a independent
      component library. This approach requires extra effort to maintain, but it's the only way to
      ensure a single source of truth and long-term maintainability. We also use **Storybook** and
      **AI Skills** to generate documentation and usage guides for team members and AI to follow to
      align on the design language and component usage.
    </TDTradeOffs>
    <TDImpact>
      Established a single source of truth that boosted AI scaffolding success rates, with **80% of
      UI generated** without manual fixes. It ensured **100% design consistency** across projects
      and allowed for a **prototype-to-production** workflow, reducing overall development time by
      **30-40%**.
    </TDImpact>
  </TechnicalDecision>
</Card>

<Card className="mt-4">
  <TechnicalDecision title="The Multi-Dimensional Range Filter">
    <TDContext>
      In the Data Warehouse project, the date picker involved heavy business logic—preset ranges
      (Month-to-Date/Year-to-Date), tiered permissions (Pro vs. Basic), and complex period
      comparisons. On the other hand, the search conditions were shared across multiple pages which
      frustrated users because their filter settings would reset whenever they navigated between
      different dashboard views.
    </TDContext>
    <TDTradeOffs>
      Instead of writing giant, messy conditional blocks, I built a dedicated Class-based filtering
      engine. I used class properties to securely store and manage the current filter state, while
      creating individual methods to handle each complex preset. This meant each method had only one
      job, making the whole logic much easier to debug and keep clean. Simultaneously, I integrated
      Pinia to manage global filter states. This allowed us to persist user selections across the
      entire application, ensuring that once a date range or comparison was set, it remained
      consistent throughout the user’s session.
    </TDTradeOffs>
    <TDImpact>
      This refactor turned a maintenance nightmare into a scalable system, reducing the time to
      implement new rules by 50%. More importantly, the persistent state significantly improved the
      UX, leading to a much smoother workflow where merchants could compare metrics across pages
      without repetitive configurations.
    </TDImpact>
  </TechnicalDecision>
</Card>

## Challenge: Team Allocation

<Card>

- **Challenge**: The project timeline was tight, and with a small team of 2 frontend developers, it was challenging to allocate resources effectively to meet deadlines without compromising on quality.
- **Solution**: Listed all features before development began, breaking them down into manageable tasks. Assigned tasks based on the urgency and each developer's time, ensuring a balanced workload, and update and adjust the plan regularly with the project manager.
